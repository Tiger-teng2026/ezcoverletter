---
title: How to Write an ATS-Friendly Cover Letter
description: A practical checklist for cover letters that survive applicant tracking systems and still read like a human wrote them.
date: 2026-08-08
---

Applicant tracking systems (ATS) are not trying to grade your prose. They scan for relevance, clarity, and keywords that match the job. The goal is a letter that a machine can parse and a recruiter can skim in under a minute.

## Lead with the role and a clear fit

Open with the exact job title and one concrete reason you belong there. Vague openers like “I am excited to apply” waste the first screen of attention.

## Mirror the job’s language—without stuffing

Pull 4–6 high-signal phrases from the posting (tools, outcomes, domains) and use them naturally. Prefer the employer’s wording over inventing synonyms the ATS may not recognize.

## Show proof, not adjectives

Replace “strong communicator” with a metric or shipped result. Numbers, timelines, and scope travel better through both ATS filters and hiring managers.

## Keep structure simple

Use short paragraphs, standard headings if needed, and a single clean sign-off. Fancy layouts, tables, and text boxes often break parsing.

## Close with a specific ask

End with a short call to action—availability for a brief call, or interest in the next step—then stop. One signature block is enough.
