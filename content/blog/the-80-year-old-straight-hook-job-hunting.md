## The Story: Fishing Without Bait

Jiang Shang was 80 years old. By any standard of his time, he was ancient, retired, and completely overlooked by the establishment. He had spent decades studying strategy, politics, and military affairs, but no ruler had ever given him a chance. He was, in modern terms, the ultimate "overqualified but overlooked" candidate.

But Jiang had a plan.

He traveled to the Wei River, near the hunting grounds of King Wen of Zhou. He sat by the riverbank with his fishing rod—but here's the twist: **his hook was completely straight, and he used no bait.**

Passersby laughed at him. *"Old man, you'll never catch a fish like that!"*

Jiang calmly replied: *"I'm not fishing for fish. I'm fishing for a king."*

Word spread. Soon, everyone in the region was talking about the crazy old man who fished with a straight hook. The story reached King Wen, a wise ruler desperately seeking talented advisors to help him build a better kingdom.

Intrigued, the king traveled to the river himself. He approached Jiang Shang and asked, *"How do you fish with a straight hook?"*

Jiang replied: *"The fish that are meant to be caught will come to me. I don't chase them. I attract them."*

King Wen was so impressed by Jiang's wisdom and unorthodox thinking that he invited Jiang to his court—and immediately appointed him as his chief military strategist. Jiang Shang went on to help King Wen's son conquer the corrupt Shang dynasty and establish the Zhou dynasty, one of the most celebrated dynasties in Chinese history.

He was 80 years old. His career hadn't even started—and then it became legendary.

## 3 Modern Career Lessons From a 3,000-Year-Old Job Seeker

Jiang Shang didn't get his dream role by begging. He didn't spam his resume to every warlord in China. He used **strategic positioning, differentiated value, and self-made visibility**.

Here's how to apply his philosophy to your 2026 job search:

### 1. Stop Spraying. Start Positioning.

Sending 500 identical applications is the equivalent of begging in the marketplace. You're invisible because you look exactly like everyone else.

Jiang Shang could have walked from castle to castle, asking for an audience. Instead, he **chose a specific location**—the river near the king's hunting grounds—and he **did something unforgettable** that would guarantee word-of-mouth marketing.

**Your move today:** Instead of applying to every job that moves, identify **10 dream companies** and go deep. Research their pain points, their recent challenges, their strategic directions. Then position yourself as the specific solution to their specific problem. Don't be a generalist—be *the* specialist they didn't know they needed.

### 2. The Hook Is Your Difference

A straight hook with no bait is useless for fishing—but it's *perfect* for getting noticed. Jiang Shang deliberately created a "pattern interrupt" that made people stop, laugh, and talk.

**Your move today:** What makes you *genuinely* different? Not your skills (everyone has skills)—but your **unique combination of experience, perspective, and approach**. When you write your cover letter or pitch, lead with that. Don't start with "I'm a hardworking team player." Start with a bold perspective, a surprising insight, or a specific result that makes a recruiter sit up and say, *"Wait—I need to know more about this person."*

### 3. Build the "Buzz" That Brings Recruiters to You

Jiang didn't chase the king—*the king came to him*. But that didn't happen by accident. He created a buzz so loud that the rumor traveled naturally.

**Your move today:** Build a **digital reputation** that makes recruiters come to you. This means:
- Publishing thoughtful content on LinkedIn or Twitter/X about your industry
- Sharing case studies or portfolio work that demonstrates your expertise
- Engaging in communities (Reddit, Discord, Slack groups) where your target employers hang out

When you consistently demonstrate value in public, you stop being a "candidate" and start being a "known expert." And known experts don't apply for jobs—they get recruited.
